Last spring, our college library started staying open all night, and the first week it was almost empty.
By the second week, though, every table was full of students with laptops, notebooks, and cold cups of tea.
Some of them were studying for exams, some were finishing projects, and a few were simply avoiding their hostel rooms.
The librarian, who had worked there for twenty years, said she had never seen the building so alive.
She began leaving small notes on the desks, wishing everyone good luck and reminding them to drink water.
Slowly, those notes became a tradition, and students started leaving notes for each other as well.
When the exams finally ended, the desks were covered with messages, drawings, and thank-you cards.
Nobody planned it, yet that quiet corner of the campus became the place where people felt looked after.
